What this is, plainly

BirdStrikeNet is a historical-analysis and airport-planning tool for Eastern Shore of Maryland general-aviation airports. It is not a real-time or operational safety tool — don't use it to make an in-the-moment go/no-go decision. It describes what has been reported in the past, not what is happening right now.

The damage-label rule

A report counts as damaging only when FAA's own INDICATED_DAMAGE field says so explicitly; not damaging only when it explicitly says no. A report whose outcome FAA itself never resolved is excluded from the damage-share denominator — it still counts in the report total, just not the percentage. In the current FAA export, INDICATED_DAMAGE happens to never be blank for the confirmed business/private fixed-wing GA population (verified against the raw 353,969-row export, not assumed) — but the code path for an unknown outcome is real and tested (tests/test_dashboard_query.py), not a hypothetical.

Small-sample thresholds

  • Small sample: fewer than 20 known-outcome reports — the number is shown, flagged, not hidden.
  • Insufficient evidence: fewer than 10 known-outcome reports — excluded from any damage ranking or "sort to top," shown as "insufficient evidence," never as 0%.

Every damage share shown carries a Wilson 95% confidence interval, not just a point estimate — at n<20 that interval is often wide enough on its own to make the small-sample flag obvious.

The FBBO banding-station limitation

FBBO's local activity numbers come from mist-net banding — nets that catch small-to-medium birds flying at low vegetation level. They structurally cannot catch geese, ducks, vultures, gulls, raptors, herons, cranes, pelicans, shorebirds, gamebirds, or deer, no matter how many of those were actually present. A "0" for one of those groups is never shown as if it meant "no local activity" — the dashboard labels it "not sampled by this method" instead, and a genuinely unmonitored month (0 observation hours) is a third, separate state, labeled "not monitored." Treat FBBO numbers as informative only for small songbirds, swifts, swallows, doves, and pigeons — exactly the taxa mist nets are built to catch.

Regulatory framing

FAA Advisory Circulars 150/5200-33/-32/-38 set mandatory standards only for Part 139-certificated (commercial-service) airports. Most Eastern Shore MD GA airports in this roster are not Part 139 airports, so every action-panel recommendation here is voluntary best practice, never a compliance requirement.
